# Laboratory II — Same matrix, three passports

**Book correspondence:** Extended Laboratory II; E02.01, E03.01, E03.04.

For $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&1\end{pmatrix}$: in $\mathbb F_2^2$ it cycles all three nonzero vectors; the same binary matrix represents multiplication by $\alpha$ on $\mathbb F_4$ regarded as a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space; after *extending scalars* to $\mathbb F_4$ it has two eigenvalues, $\alpha$ and $\alpha^2$.

Then distinguish scalar *restriction* from scalar *extension*: the first turns one $\mathbb F_4$-dimension into two $\mathbb F_2$-dimensions; the second takes a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_2$-space to a two-dimensional $\mathbb F_4$-space.
